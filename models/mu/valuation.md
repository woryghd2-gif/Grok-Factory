As of 2026-10-25. Horizon: 12 months, to 2027-10-25. Units: US$ millions except per share and percents. Generated by `models/mu/build_model.py`. The market price is a reference. It is not an input to the target. No rating.

## 1. Price target

## 2. Bridge from the model

The unlevered cash flows are built from these forecast lines and from nothing else:

- EBIT, from `income.md`. NOPAT = EBIT × (1 − 15.5%). The 15.5% is applied to EBIT, not to pretax income, so the small interest tax shield is not in the unlevered flow.
- Depreciation, the memo line in `income.md`, added back.
- Net capex, from `cashflow.md`, subtracted. This matches the company's net-of-incentives definition.
- Change in operating net working capital: receivables + inventories + other current assets − accounts payable and accrued expenses − other current liabilities. Contract liabilities, debt, cash, and marketable investments are outside this change.
- Stock-based compensation is already inside EBIT and is not added back.

Equity value at the valuation date = enterprise value − debt carrying value + cash, restricted cash, and marketable investments − noncurrent customer contract liabilities − noncurrent income taxes payable. All of those balances are the September 3, 2026 amounts. A balance sheet between September 3 and October 25 was not obtained.

Contract liabilities are subtracted because the cash is in the cash balance and the liability is the customers' claim. Deposits are not free cash flow, which is why the repayment is not also deducted inside unlevered free cash flow. The noncurrent tax payable is subtracted for the same reason: the forecast pays new book tax in cash and does not pay this opening payable again.

NOPAT + depreciation − net capex − the increase in operating net working capital equals unlevered free cash flow. The FY2030E column is the normalized year. It is not added again on top of the terminal value. The terminal value is that year's flow grown at g, valued at the FY2029E year-end.

### Terminal method

Gordon growth at g = 3.0%. The cap is 4.0%, the September 16, 2026 FOMC longer-run median real GDP growth of 2.0% plus longer-run PCE inflation of 2.0% (the sum, not the compound). g is 100 basis points inside that cap because memory revenue over a long period is bits up and price down, so it should not sit on the nominal-GDP ceiling. https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/fomcprojtabl20260916.htm

The terminal year is not FY2029E grown at 3%. FY2029E is only the first price cut (−30% ASP). A mid-cycle year is rebuilt. Bits grow at g. Cash cost per bit falls another 5%. Depreciation and capex are the steady state that grows net PP&E at g (capex = (16% + g) / 92% × opening PP&E, which is the algebra of the depreciation rule). Revenue is then set so GAAP gross margin equals 45.0%. That margin is five points above FY2025A gross margin of 39.8%, the only pre-spike year in the obtained set, and it is far below FY2026A at 80.7%. The five points are the model's allowance for the company's statement that contract floors hold gross margin meaningfully above the prior cycle peak. The floors themselves were not obtained, so 45% is not a company figure. R&D and SG&A grow at g off the FY2029E level. The terminal gross margin asserted in the script is exactly 45%.

Terminal value at the FY2029E year-end = FY2030E unlevered free cash flow / (WACC − g) = 270,482. Its present value is 182,591, which is **31.7% of enterprise value**.

## 7. What would move the target

The base 12-month value is $620.28. The larger recorded swing in section 4 is $290 per share across the primary grid.

- **Mid-cycle gross margin.** This is the terminal price reset. Floors on the strategic agreements were not obtained. A margin nearer FY2026A, or nearer FY2024A, moves the target by more than a 100-basis-point change in g.
- **WACC, mostly through beta.** The 2.23 beta and the July 1, 2026 ERP are two different dates and, on beta, two vendors disagree. Debt is too small for the missing bond yield to matter.
- **FY2027E revenue path.** The year is anchored on a $61.5 billion first quarter and on sequential growth. A flat year after that quarter is a lower revenue path than the one in `segments.md`. The model does not print that case as the base, because the company said each quarter grows.
- **FY2027E–FY2029E net capex.** Only the first half of FY2027E is guided ($25 billion). The $55 / $46 / $40 billion path is a model bridge. Higher capex cuts explicit free cash flow and raises steady-state terminal capex once the PP&E base is larger.
- **ASP in FY2029E.** The −30% cut is the first normalization, not a return to FY2025A prices. A full give-back of the FY2026A price spike is a different case and was not run. HBM dollars, which would change the CMBU cost gap, were not obtained.
- **A discretionary buyback.** The model buys back nothing. Spending the $35.16 billion authorization would cut the share count and the cash in the bridge. That program is capacity, not a forecast, and it is not in the target.
- **Paying the $9.82 billion tax payable, or repaying deposits faster than one-third in FY2029E.** Both are already subtracted at face in the bridge, so faster repayment does not change the target unless the cash was going to earn the 4.44% yield for longer. The yield on that timing is second order.
