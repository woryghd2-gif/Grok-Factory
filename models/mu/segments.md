It is not a third independent assumption.

## CMBU

Hyperscale DRAM and all HBM. FY2027E bits +28%: company DRAM supply is guided in line with industry growth in the mid-20s for calendar 2026 and the low-20s for calendar 2027–2028, and HBM bits grow faster than conventional DRAM through calendar 2028, so this unit is above the company DRAM rate. Cash cost per bit −5%, three points less deflation than CDBU and MCBU, because a higher HBM mix uses more wafers per bit. That gap is the HBM-mix driver. HBM revenue dollars were not obtained, so mix is not a revenue split. FY2028E holds the FY2027E exit price (the small ASP change versus the FY2027E average is only the intra-year ramp of +2.5% a quarter) and grows bits 22%. FY2029E cuts ASP 30% as ID1 is in volume and ID2, Hiroshima, and the Singapore NAND fab start, and grows bits 18%.

## CDBU

Data-center SSDs and NAND components, plus OEM, enterprise, and NeoCloud DRAM. FY2027E bits +40%: FQ4 data-center SSD revenue was nearly $10 billion and more than 10× the prior year, so a large part of the exit rate is volume that was not in the FY2026A average. Annualizing that ramp, plus NAND bits still growing, is a faster bit year than the company-wide NAND comment (Micron NAND supply grows less than the industry). Cash cost per bit −8% (G8/G9 bit cost, not a disclosed cost-per-bit). FY2028E bits +20% at the FY2027E exit price. FY2029E bits +18% and ASP −30%, same cleanroom timing as CMBU. Share of the data-center SSD market was not obtained.

## MCBU

Smartphones, client PCs, and component DRAM and NAND. FY2026A bits fell because wafers moved to other units. FY2027E bits +6%: the allocation does not fully reverse while data-center demand is still short, and 1γ mobile is shipping. ASP does the rest of the revenue bridge. Cash cost per bit −8%. FY2028E bits +10% once ID1 wafers (mid-calendar 2027) start to ease the allocation. FY2029E bits +12% and ASP −30%.
