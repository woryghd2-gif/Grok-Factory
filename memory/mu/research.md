## 1. Node map

Micron designs, manufactures, and sells volatile DRAM (including HBM) and non-volatile NAND and NOR. Customers pay Micron; Micron pays equipment vendors, materials suppliers, employees, governments (tax, net of incentives), and debtholders. The cash that arrived in FY2026A did not all come from recognized sales: customers also posted deposits against future supply.

**Who pays whom [FACT, FY2026 10-K Item 1 and Note 16].** End demand is servers and AI accelerators, smartphones, PCs, autos, and industrial devices. The payer on Micron’s invoice is a hyperscale cloud customer, an OEM server/enterprise/NeoCloud customer, a smartphone or PC OEM, an auto or embedded OEM, or a distributor. Micron sells mostly direct, and also through distributors, retailers, and independent reps. Ordinary contracts are short; payment is due shortly after delivery. Strategic customer agreements (SCAs) are multi-year take-or-pay contracts. Under those, customers have posted cash deposits and letters of credit. Deposits are a liability, not revenue, and are returned if the customer meets the minimum purchase commitment.

**What would break it [VIEW, mechanisms from the 10-K and the September 30, 2026 prepared remarks].**

1. **Price.** FY2026A growth was mostly ASP, not bits. A mid-20% bit increase cannot defend revenue if ASP gives back the approximate 180% (DRAM) or 200% (NAND) rise. SCA floors are supposed to hold gross margin “meaningfully above” any prior cycle peak even at the floor **[FACT, company statement, 10-K MD&A]**. The floors are not published, and about a quarter of the SCA revenue the company estimates through 2030 has pricing that is still negotiated off the market **[FACT, prepared remarks]**.
2. **A few buyers pausing.** No customer was 10% or more of FY2026A revenue, but the top ten were about half of revenue in each of the last three years, and one customer was 17% of FY2025A revenue, mostly in CMBU. Data-center memory is the growth node. An AI-capex pause hits CMBU and CDBU first and, because Micron has been moving bits toward those units, it hits the redirected mobile and client supply as well.
3. **Supply arriving.** The company says it does not have line of sight to DRAM supply-demand balance, and that cleanroom additions are required to narrow the gap. Its own new DRAM cleanrooms become meaningful only a few quarters after first wafer output (mid-calendar 2027 at the earliest for ID1). Competitors, including CXMT, can add DRAM bits. The 10-K names that risk and does not quantify their capacity. Share figures: `not obtained`.
4. **The deposits are not profit.** $12.9 billion is customers’ cash. It comes back between 2029 and 2031 if they buy what they committed to. If Micron cannot deliver, or a contract breaks, the liability and the relationship both become a cash and revenue problem. If they do buy, the cash leaves.
5. **Technology qualification.** HBM attach is a design-in. Missing a platform (the remarks name an NVIDIA custom HBM4E, “NVHBM”) would strand mix in a lower-price conventional DRAM market at the same time the company is spending to add cleanroom.
6. **A legal or trade stop.** A German court on October 6, 2026 ordered Micron to stop offering, marketing, importing, or possessing specified 3D NAND products in Germany (Note 11). Export controls can cut China or specific customers. Neither is sized in dollars in the 10-K.

## 2. Business model

**What they sell [FACT, FY2026 10-K Item 1].** Semiconductor memory and storage: DRAM (DDR, LPDDR, GDDR, HBM), NAND (components, SSDs, managed NAND, multi-chip packages), and NOR. Forms include components, modules, SSDs, managed NAND, multi-chip packages, and wafers. Brands: Micron, and private label. The FY2025 earnings release still named the Crucial retail brand; the FY2026 10-K does not. Whether Crucial remains a selling brand: `not obtained` from the FY2026 10-K.

**Who pays [FACT].** Large hyperscale cloud customers and all data-center HBM buyers (CMBU). Data-center SSD and NAND-component buyers, plus OEM, enterprise, and NeoCloud DRAM buyers (CDBU). Smartphone and client-PC OEMs, plus consumer and component DRAM/NAND (MCBU). Automotive, industrial, and consumer embedded buyers (AEBU). Distributors carry inventory and also sell competitors’ parts.

**Pricing [FACT].** Ordinary contracts are short. SCA pricing is fixed or negotiated periodically. A majority of SCAs have minimum and maximum price bands. Payments on deliveries are generally due shortly after shipment. There is no published list price and no single “deal size.” The obtained deal-scale figures are the portfolio of SCAs, not a per-contract ASP:

**Economics of a delivery [FACT].** Revenue is recognized when control transfers, generally at a point in time. Deposits are not a significant financing component; they secure capacity. If the customer misses the minimum, Micron may keep some or all of the deposit and recognize it as revenue. Price protection to distributors is a reduction of revenue.

### 3.2 What moves each segment

**CMBU [FACT, 10-K MD&A and Item 1].** Memory for large hyperscale cloud customers, and HBM for every data-center customer. Also DDR, LPDDR, and GDDR. FY2026A sales were primarily DDR5, HBM, and LPDDR5. Revenue +219% versus FY2025A on ASP and bits. FY2025A revenue +257% on DRAM bits and ASP, with supply shifted toward this unit. FQ4 FY2026A revenue $16,283, +18% sequential, on higher price and bits. Gross margin 83%, flat sequentially, because higher price was offset by a higher HBM mix **[FACT, prepared remarks]**. That is the evidence in hand that HBM, at FQ4 mix, was dilutive to CMBU gross margin even while HBM revenue grew faster than the company. Management also said calendar-2027 HBM supply is mostly agreed, at significant year-over-year price increases, and that the gross-margin gap versus conventional DRAM is narrowing. HBM revenue dollars and HBM bit share: `not obtained`.

**CDBU [FACT].** Data-center SSDs and NAND components for all data-center customers, plus DRAM for OEM, enterprise, and NeoCloud customers (AI cloud providers that are not traditional hyperscalers). DRAM sales were primarily DDR5 and DDR4. FY2026A revenue +420% on ASP and bits. FY2025A revenue +45% on data-center DRAM and NAND ASP and on NAND bits (data-center SSDs). FQ4 revenue $18,002, +56% sequential, gross margin 90%. Prepared remarks: data-center SSD revenue in FQ4 was nearly $10 billion, more than 10 times the year-ago quarter, and over two-thirds of company NAND revenue. Company expects a fifth consecutive calendar year of record data-center SSD share in calendar 2026. The share percent: `not obtained`.

**MCBU [FACT].** Smartphones, client PCs, consumer DRAM, and component DRAM and NAND (TLC and QLC). Mobile is LPDDR. FY2026A revenue +209% on ASP, partly offset by lower bits because supply was moved to other units. FY2025A revenue +2%: DRAM ASP up and DRAM bits down for the same allocation reason; NAND bits up and NAND ASP down. FQ4 revenue $13,114, +14% sequential, on price, with bits down again. Gross margin 90%. Nearly half of FQ4 MCBU revenue was 1γ product **[FACT, prepared remarks]**.

**AEBU [FACT].** Automotive, industrial, and consumer embedded: discrete and module DRAM, discrete and managed NAND, SSDs, NOR. FY2026A revenue +234% on ASP and bits. FY2025A revenue +3% on bits, with ASP down on legacy products. FQ4 revenue $6,824, +47% sequential, gross margin 84%, on price and bits. Content commentary in the remarks (Level 4+ vehicles typically above 200GB of memory and multiple terabytes of storage) is a company description, not a TAM.

## 7. Industry

**Structure [FACT, FY2026 10-K Item 1].** Memory and storage are competitive, cyclical, and capital-intensive. The named competitors are Samsung Electronics, SK hynix, Kioxia, Sandisk, CXMT, and YMTC. Some are conglomerates that the 10-K says may have a larger share, more resources, and lower labor and compliance costs. Share percents are not in the 10-K. Consolidation or new entrants are listed as risks. Micron’s own strategy sentence is to advance process technology, raise bits per wafer, and add DRAM wafer capacity in the second half of the decade because node transitions alone will not cover demand.

**DRAM / NAND / HBM economics [FACT as company description].**

- Bit cost falls when the node shrinks (1γ DRAM with EUV; G9 NAND). Cost per bit is not disclosed.
- HBM uses more wafer bits than the same capacity of conventional DRAM because of the stack, the logic base die, and packaging. The company said in FQ4 that a higher HBM mix held CMBU gross margin flat despite higher prices, and that calendar-2027 HBM price increases are narrowing that gap. Trade ratio of wafers per HBM bit: `not obtained`.
- Supply responds slowly. Cleanroom space, not just tools, is the constraint the company emphasizes. Equipment spend can be pulled forward inside existing cleanrooms; new shells (Idaho, New York, Hiroshima, Singapore NAND, Taiwan second cleanroom) hit volume in calendar 2027–2030.
- Price is set by the shortage and, increasingly, by contract bands. The company said on September 30, 2026 that it does not have line of sight to when DRAM supply and demand rebalance, even with announced industry cleanroom plans, and that calendar 2027 and 2028 should be tighter than calendar 2026.
- Buyers have started to act like capacity is strategic: take-or-pay volume, price bands, and deposits. That is a change from a spot or short-contract memory cycle. It is visible in the contract-liability line.

**TAM.** A dollar total addressable market is `not obtained` from a primary document. The company does publish industry bit-shipment growth ranges (section 3.3) and server unit growth. Those are Micron’s forecasts, not a third-party TAM. No third-party TAM is used here.

Long-lived assets at September 3, 2026 **[FACT, Note 22]**: Taiwan $27,613, United States $14,463, Singapore $12,345, Japan $7,247, Malaysia $1,184, India $609, China $473, other $97. Production is mostly outside the United States. Customer headquarters are mostly in the United States. That split is the geopolitical node.
